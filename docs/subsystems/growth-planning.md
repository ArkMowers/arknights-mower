# Operator Growth Planning

## 1. Interfaces

The existing recommendation route lists owned four-, five- and six-star mastery-capable operators regardless of promotion, including completed operators, and operators with modules. Each skill exposes target-specific costs for mastery levels one through three. Module choices include opening and upgrades to levels one, two or three, default to the available maximum, and retain already-opened modules with unfinished upgrades; promotion choices target elite two level one, module level (60/50/40), or the rarity-specific maximum (90/80/70); the highest selected prerequisite level is counted once. The three explicit level targets are mutually exclusive. Skill and module selections automatically show their minimum unsatisfied level prerequisite; a higher explicit target remains selected. Level/promotion, basic skills one through seven, mastery and module upgrade costs render separately, while the aggregate budget still deduplicates prerequisites. The required crafting fee is reported independently from those direct upgrade costs and is included once in the total LMD requirement. EXP uses the warehouse EXP image. Modules display expanded. The mastery plan API admits planning requests before readiness but dispatch requires elite two and basic skill level seven. A terminal mastery stage never switches to a halving assistant for a later stage.

`/growth-plan` stores promotion and module selections. `/mastery-t3-summary` combines them with skill plans and counts promotion, level and basic-skill prerequisites once per operator. Confirmed paid training stages are excluded. Unknown prerequisite data returns a visible error. Module goals persist their selected target level and charge only unfinished stages.

`/mastery-plan/start` verifies the selected skill target against confirmed local finished-material stock; craftable ingredients do not satisfy immediate execution readiness. It requires elite two, basic skill level seven, enabled mastery automation, a running scheduler, and no conflicting scheduled operator or occupied mastery queue. It inserts one start task keyed by the database plan id and wakes the scheduler. Repeated requests for the same queued plan return its existing queue status. Other active training, pending mastery work or a different unfinished plan waiting for materials receives an explicit blocking reason. The executor still verifies the training room before starting. Only an observed inserted or existing queue task receives a success response; an active training plan, missing materials and unavailable execution remain distinct responses.

## 2. Materials and Farming

Available stock is reserved by operator. The workshop request prepares only the next missing recipe of that operator, bounds output by its shortage and affordable batches, and recalculates after each confirmed batch using local inventory. Output upper limits are current inventory plus the affordable outstanding quantity; lower limits remain reserved inventory thresholds. It adds no unrelated stockpiling or fodder. Nine-Colored Deer requires separate fodder and is excluded from missing-only allocation. A recipe change queues continuation with remaining mood; ordinary first-time dispatch retains its normal mood gate. Existing manual backup, generation and material protection contracts remain binding. Shared inventory retains confirmed crafting changes across delayed cloud requests: pending per-item baselines and change directions survive restart. For an item changed only by production, cloud counts at or above the predicted inventory can reconcile; for consumption alone, counts at or below the prediction can reconcile. An item both produced and consumed requires exact equality with predicted inventory, or a newer actual warehouse observation covering that item. Unknown directions from migrated records also require exact equality. A missing item in a partial scan is not an observed zero. HTTP request time alone is insufficient evidence of fresh stock.

Chips never become workshop recipes. Each missing dual chip adds two same-profession chip packs and one catalyst to the displayed manual requirements. Each missing catalyst adds ninety purchase certificates. Existing dual chips and catalysts reduce derived requirements before certificates are counted. These are final products and their manual ingredients, not independent additional uses.

`/growth-chip-farming` updates the active MAA weekly plan only: outstanding small-chip and chip-pack requirements bind the corresponding PR stage; purchase certificates bind AP-5. Target stock is total remaining need, not merely the shortage. Related old rules are replaced; unrelated limits, stage order and plan fields are preserved. Affected ratio members are removed so they cannot suppress required stages. Required stages follow annihilation and retain normal stage-availability handling.

## 3. Subsystem Invariants

- **[INV-GROWTH-01] Shared Growth Budget**: Growth planning admits unready operators without dispatching training, counts shared prerequisites once, reserves stock by operator, emits only outstanding permitted recipes, and keeps manual chip conversion separate from automatic crafting.

- **[INV-STOCK-01] Confirmed Workshop Inventory**: A later cloud request timestamp alone never replaces locally confirmed crafting changes. Pending outputs and consumed ingredients remain protected until single-direction cloud counts reach the predicted inventory in that direction, or a newer actual inventory read reconciles them. Items both produced and consumed require exact predicted cloud counts or a newer actual inventory read. Workshop upper and lower limits remain absolute inventory thresholds.

## 4. Observations and Persistence

Statistics count selected four-, five- and six-star operators from the full owned roster: elite-two maximum level, module level threshold or above, elite-two total, opened modules and skills at mastery three. Opened modules count equipment ids once and exclude default equipment. Historical snapshots record real synchronization timestamps, retain at most 3000 observations, and display trends only with multiple observations. Current data does not invent historical points. Historical snapshots without a metric do not contribute invented zeros to that metric's trend. Goal and history files participate in configuration backup.

The sixth card values completed promotion, leveling, basic skills, mastery and module stages using the pinned offline [Yituliu item-value snapshot](../../arknights_mower/data/growth_values.json). Its metadata records the source commit, retrieval date and [algorithm reference](https://ark.yituliu.cn/docs/item-value-algorithm). Quantities are reconstructed from observed progression and multiplied by published item values; current inventory and unfinished goals are excluded. EXP uses the intermediate battle record value divided by 1000. Shared progression across operator forms is counted once. Integrated-strategy progression does not consume ordinary warehouse materials. This valuation estimates standard material investment, not actual sanity spent, and cannot reconstruct historical promotion tickets or other discounts. Unpriced materials and missing progression definitions are listed explicitly; the card labels incomplete coverage as a priced subtotal.

The sanity card also reports direct completed-growth LMD and EXP consumption, plus equivalent natural-regeneration days at 240 sanity per day. Historical crafting fees cannot be inferred from progression and are not added to the LMD subtotal.

The opened-module card also reports cumulative module data blocks spent across completed module stages. The mastery card reports cumulative skill summaries from completed basic-skill and mastery upgrades as volume-three equivalents: volume one / 9 + volume two / 3 + volume three, without crafting byproducts. These auxiliary totals follow the same selected rarity filters as the six cards.
